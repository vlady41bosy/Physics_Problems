### **Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution**

#### **1. Identify the Formula and Given Values**

The magnetic field ($B$) generated by a long, straight wire at a distance $r$ is given by Ampere's Law:


$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$$

* **Currents ($I_1$ and $I_2$):** $5 \text{ A}$ each.
* **Total distance between wires:** $10 \text{ cm} = 0.10 \text{ m}$.
* **Distance to midpoint ($r$):** The midpoint is exactly half the total distance, so $r = 5 \text{ cm} = 0.05 \text{ m}$ for both wires.
* **Permeability of free space ($\mu_0$):** $4\pi \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}\cdot\text{m/A}$.

#### **2. Calculate the Field from One Wire**

Let's find the magnitude of the field created by just the first wire ($B_1$) at the midpoint.


$$B_1 = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \cdot 5}{2\pi \cdot 0.05}$$

Cancel out the $2\pi$ from the top and bottom:


$$B_1 = \frac{(2 \times 10^{-7}) \cdot 5}{0.05}$$

$$B_1 = \frac{10 \times 10^{-7}}{0.05}$$

$$B_1 = 200 \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}$$

$$B_1 = 2.0 \times 10^{-5} \text{ T}$$

#### **3. Calculate the Total Field ($B_{\text{net}}$)**

Because the second wire has the exact same current and is at the exact same distance from the midpoint, it creates a magnetic field of the exact same magnitude:


$$B_2 = 2.0 \times 10^{-5} \text{ T}$$

As established by the Right-Hand Rule, because the currents are opposite, the fields point in the same direction at the midpoint. Therefore, we simply add their magnitudes together:


$$B_{\text{net}} = B_1 + B_2$$

$$B_{\text{net}} = (2.0 \times 10^{-5}) + (2.0 \times 10^{-5})$$

$$B_{\text{net}} = 4.0 \times 10^{-5} \text{ T}$$
