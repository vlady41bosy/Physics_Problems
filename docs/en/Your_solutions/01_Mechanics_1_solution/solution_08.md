## 8. Circular Motion Solutions

To calculate the centripetal acceleration of a person standing on the equator, we can use the rotational kinematics of the Earth. 

* **Determine the angular velocity of the Earth:**
    First, we need to find the Earth's angular velocity $\omega$. The Earth completes one full rotation ($2\pi$ radians) in a time period $T$ of one day. To use standard SI units, we must convert this period into seconds:
    $$T = 24 \text{ hours} \times 60 \text{ minutes} \times 60 \text{ seconds} = 86,400 \text{ s}$$
    Now, calculate the angular velocity:
    $$\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{2\pi}{86400} \approx 7.272 \times 10^{-5} \text{ rad/s}$$

* **Calculate the centripetal acceleration:**
    The formula for centripetal acceleration $a_c$ in terms of angular velocity is:
    $$a_c = \omega^2 r$$
    Before calculating, ensure the Earth's radius $r$ is in meters to match the standard units:
    $$r = 6378 \text{ km} = 6,378,000 \text{ m}$$
    Substitute the known values into the acceleration equation:
    $$a_c = (7.272 \times 10^{-5})^2 \times 6,378,000$$
    $$a_c \approx (5.288 \times 10^{-9}) \times 6,378,000$$
    $$a_c \approx 0.0337 \text{ m/s}^2$$
    This is a relatively small acceleration compared to standard gravity ($9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$), but it is constantly acting toward the center of the Earth to keep a person moving in that circular path.
