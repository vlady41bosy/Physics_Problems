### Understanding Momentum

Think of **momentum** as a way to measure "how hard it is to stop a moving object." To find it, you only need to look at two things:
1.  **Mass ($m$):** How heavy the object is.
2.  **Velocity ($v$):** How fast the object is moving.

The formula for momentum ($p$) is just multiplying those two things together:
$$p = m \cdot v$$

*Note: In physics math, we always measure mass in kilograms (kg). Because our objects are weighed in grams, we first have to divide by 1,000 to turn them into kilograms.*

---

### Step 1: The Fly
The fly is moving very fast, but it weighs almost nothing. 

* **Mass:** 2 grams (Divide by 1,000 to get **0.002 kg**)
* **Velocity:** **10 m/s**
* **The Math:** Let's multiply them together.
    $$p = 0.002 \cdot 10$$
* **Fly's Momentum:** **0.02 kg·m/s**

### Step 2: The Tennis Ball
The tennis ball is moving slowly, but it is much heavier than the fly.

* **Mass:** 60 grams (Divide by 1,000 to get **0.060 kg**)
* **Velocity:** **1 m/s**
* **The Math:** Let's multiply them together.
    $$p = 0.060 \cdot 1$$
* **Ball's Momentum:** **0.06 kg·m/s**

---

### The Final Comparison

Now we just look at our two final numbers side-by-side:
**0.06** is bigger than **0.02**. 

Therefore, the **tennis ball has more momentum**. Even though the fly is buzzing around 10 times faster than the ball is rolling, the tennis ball is 30 times heavier. In this case, the heavy weight of the ball easily makes up for its slow speed!
