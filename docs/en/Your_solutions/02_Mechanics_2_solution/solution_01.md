
To solve these, we use the standard formula for a simple pendulum's period:

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$

Where:
* **$T$** = Period (the time it takes for one full swing).
* **$L$** = Length of the string.
* **$g$** = Gravitational acceleration.

---

### 1. Gravitational Dependence (The Moon)

**The Logic:** Gravity is the force that pulls the pendulum back to the center. If gravity is weaker, that pull is weaker, so the pendulum swings more slowly. This means the period ($T$) gets longer.

**The Calculation:**
1.  On the Moon, gravity ($g$) is $1/6$ of Earth's.
2.  In our formula, $g$ is under a square root and at the bottom (denominator). So, if gravity is divided by 6, the period is multiplied by $\sqrt{6}$.
3.  $\sqrt{6} \approx 2.45$.
4.  $4 \text{ seconds} \times 2.45 \approx 9.8 \text{ seconds}$.

**Answer:** The period on the Moon would be approximately **9.8 seconds**. It would look like it's moving in "slow motion" compared to Earth.

---

### 2. Required Length for a 1-Second Period

**The Logic:** We want to find the exact length ($L$) needed to make the pendulum finish a swing in exactly 1 second on Earth ($g \approx 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$).

**The Calculation:**
We rearrange the formula to solve for $L$:
$$L = \frac{g \cdot T^2}{4\pi^2}$$

1.  Plug in $T = 1$: $1^2 = 1$.
2.  Plug in $g = 9.81$:
3.  $L = \frac{9.81 \cdot 1}{4 \cdot (3.14)^2} \approx \frac{9.81}{39.48} \approx 0.248 \text{ meters}$.

**Answer:** The length needs to be approximately **0.25 meters** (or **25 centimeters**).

---


* **Lower Gravity** $\rightarrow$ Slower swing $\rightarrow$ **Longer Period**.
* **Shorter String** $\rightarrow$ Faster swing $\rightarrow$ **Shorter Period**.
