### **Step-by-Step Solution**

**1. State the Relevant Formula**
Electric current $I(t)$ is defined as the rate at which charge $Q(t)$ flows through a surface over time. Mathematically, it is the first derivative of the charge function with respect to time:


$$I(t) = \frac{dQ(t)}{dt}$$

**2. Find the Derivative of the Charge Function**
Given the charge function $Q(t) = 5t^2 + 5$, we take the derivative with respect to $t$:


$$I(t) = \frac{d}{dt}(5t^2 + 5)$$


Using the power rule for derivatives:


$$I(t) = 10t$$

This equation tells us the current at any given second.

**3. Calculate the Current at $t = 3$ s**
Substitute $t = 3$ into our current equation:


$$I(3) = 10(3)$$

$$I(3) = 30 \text{ A}$$

**Answer:** The current flowing through the wire at $t = 3\text{ s}$ is **30 Amperes**.
