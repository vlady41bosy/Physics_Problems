**1. Identify the Given Information**

* Current equation ($I(t)$): $2\sin(120\pi t)$
* Peak current ($I_0$): $2 \text{ A}$
* Resistance ($R$): $50\ \Omega$

**2. State the Relevant Formula**
For a purely resistive AC circuit, Ohm's Law applies instantaneously at any given time $t$. In a resistor, voltage and current are perfectly in phase, meaning there is no phase shift added to the sine wave argument.

The formula is:


$$V(t) = I(t) \cdot R$$

**3. Calculate the Voltage Equation**
Substitute the given current equation and the resistance into Ohm's Law:


$$V(t) = [2\sin(120\pi t)] \cdot 50$$

Multiply the peak current amplitude by the resistance:


$$V(t) = (2 \cdot 50) \cdot \sin(120\pi t)$$

$$V(t) = 100\sin(120\pi t)$$

**Answer:** The equation for the voltage across the resistor is **$V(t) = 100\sin(120\pi t)$**.
