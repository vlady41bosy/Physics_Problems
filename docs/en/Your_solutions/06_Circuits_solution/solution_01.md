Here is the step-by-step mathematical solution for both circuit configurations.

### **Case 1: Resistors in Series**

**1. Calculate Equivalent Resistance**
In a series circuit, the equivalent resistance ($R_{eq}$) is the sum of the individual resistances:


$$R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$$

$$R_{eq} = 15 + 30 + 50$$

$$R_{eq} = 95 \text{ \Omega}$$

**2. Calculate Total Current**
Using Ohm's Law ($V = I \cdot R$), we can find the current flowing from the battery:


$$I = \frac{V}{R_{eq}}$$

$$I = \frac{12}{95}$$

$$I \approx 0.126 \text{ A}$$

**Series Answers:**

* **Equivalent Resistance:** 95 Ω
* **Total Current:** ~0.126 A (or 126 mA)

### **Case 2: Resistors in Parallel**

**1. Calculate Equivalent Resistance**
In a parallel circuit, the reciprocal of the equivalent resistance is the sum of the reciprocals of the individual resistances:


$$\frac{1}{R_{eq}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3}$$

$$\frac{1}{R_{eq}} = \frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{30} + \frac{1}{50}$$

To add these fractions, find the common denominator, which is 150:


$$\frac{1}{R_{eq}} = \frac{10}{150} + \frac{5}{150} + \frac{3}{150}$$

$$\frac{1}{R_{eq}} = \frac{18}{150}$$

Now, flip the fraction to solve for $R_{eq}$:


$$R_{eq} = \frac{150}{18}$$

$$R_{eq} = 8.33 \text{ \Omega}$$

**2. Calculate Total Current**
Using Ohm's Law with the exact fraction to avoid rounding errors:


$$I = \frac{V}{R_{eq}}$$

$$I = \frac{12}{\frac{150}{18}}$$

$$I = 12 \cdot \frac{18}{150}$$

$$I = \frac{216}{150}$$

$$I = 1.44 \text{ A}$$

**Parallel Answers:**

* **Equivalent Resistance:** 8.33 Ω
* **Total Current:** 1.44 A
