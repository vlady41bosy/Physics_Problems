**Answer:**
The magnetic force on the wire for each angle is:
*   **a) $90^\circ$:** $10 \text{ N}$
*   **b) $45^\circ$:** $7.07 \text{ N}$
*   **c) $0^\circ$:** $0 \text{ N}$

**Short Explanation:**
The magnetic force (often called the Laplace force) on a straight wire depends on how the wire is aligned with the magnetic field. The force is strongest when the wire cuts directly across the field lines at a perfect right angle ($90^\circ$). As you tilt the wire, it cuts through fewer field lines, reducing the force. If the wire is completely parallel to the field lines ($0^\circ$), it simply slides along them without experiencing any magnetic push at all. 

***

### **Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution**

#### **1. Identify the Formula and Given Values**
The magnitude of the magnetic force on a straight, current-carrying wire is given by the formula:
$$F = I L B \sin(\theta)$$

Where:
*   **$I$ (Current):** $10 \text{ A}$
*   **$L$ (Length):** $2.0 \text{ m}$
*   **$B$ (Magnetic Field):** $0.5 \text{ T}$
*   **$\theta$ (Angle):** The angle between the wire and the magnetic field.

Before calculating for specific angles, we can calculate the constant part of the equation ($ILB$):
$$I \cdot L \cdot B = 10 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 0.5 = 10 \text{ N}$$

So, the master equation for this specific wire is:
$$F = 10 \sin(\theta)$$

#### **2. Calculate for Case A: $90^\circ$**
When the wire is perpendicular to the field:

$$F = 10 \sin(90^\circ)$$

Since $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$:

$$F = 10 \cdot 1 = 10 \text{ N}$$

#### **3. Calculate for Case B: $45^\circ$**
When the wire is at a $45^\circ$ angle:

$$F = 10 \sin(45^\circ)$$

Since $\sin(45^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx 0.707$:

$$F = 10 \cdot 0.707 = 7.07 \text{ N}$$

#### **4. Calculate for Case C: $0^\circ$**
When the wire is perfectly parallel to the field:
$$F = 10 \sin(0^\circ)$$
Since $\sin(0^\circ) = 0$:
$$F = 10 \cdot 0 = 0 \text{ N}$$
