

### **Step-by-Step Solution**

**1. Identify the Given Information**
* Length of the string ($L$): $64 \text{ cm} = 0.64 \text{ m}$
* 
* Fundamental frequency ($f_1$): $330 \text{ Hz}$

**2. State the Relevant Formulas**
For a string fixed at both ends, the fundamental frequency (first harmonic, with one antinode) creates a standing wave where the length of the string is equal to exactly half of the wavelength ($\lambda$). 

$$L = \frac{\lambda}{2}$$

Therefore, the wavelength is twice the length of the string:

$$\lambda = 2L$$

The speed of a wave ($v$) is the product of its frequency and wavelength:

$$v = f \cdot \lambda$$

**3. Calculate the Wavelength**
Substitute the length of the string (in meters) into the wavelength formula:

$$\lambda = 2 \cdot 0.64$$

$$\lambda = 1.28 \text{ m}$$

**4. Calculate the Speed of the Wave**
Substitute the frequency and the calculated wavelength into the wave speed formula:

$$v = 330 \cdot 1.28$$

$$v = 422.4 \text{ m/s}$$

**Answer:** The speed of the wave on the guitar string is **422.4 m/s**.
