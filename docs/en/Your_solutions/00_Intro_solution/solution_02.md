## 2. Systems of Equations (Step-by-Step)

**The Problem:**
Find the values of $x$ and $y$ that satisfy:

1. $2x + 3y = 12$
2. $x - y = 1$

### Step 1: Isolate one variable

We look for the simplest equation to rearrange. Equation (2) is perfect because $x$ and $y$ have no coefficients. Let's solve for $x$:


$$x = y + 1$$

### Step 2: Substitute into the other equation

Now, we take our new definition of $x$ (which is $y+1$) and plug it into Equation (1) where the $x$ used to be.

> **Note:** Always substitute into the equation you *didn't* use in Step 1.

$$2(y + 1) + 3y = 12$$

### Step 3: Solve for $y$

Now we have an equation with only one variable. Let's do the math:

* **Distribute the 2:** $2y + 2 + 3y = 12$
* **Combine like terms ($2y + 3y$):** $5y + 2 = 12$
* **Subtract 2 from both sides:** $5y = 10$
* **Divide by 5:** $\mathbf{y = 2}$

### Step 4: Solve for $x$

Now that we know $y = 2$, we plug it back into our simplest equation from Step 1:


$$x = 2 + 1$$

$$\mathbf{x = 3}$$

---

### Step 5: The "Double Check" (Verification)

 Let’s plug both numbers into the first equation to see if it holds up:


$$2(3) + 3(2) = 12$$

$$6 + 6 = 12$$


**$12 = 12$** (It works!)
