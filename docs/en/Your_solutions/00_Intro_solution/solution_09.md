

## 9. Optimization Problem (Step-by-Step)

**The Problem:**
A rectangle is tucked under the curve $y = 3 - x^2$ in the first quadrant (where $x$ and $y$ are both positive). What dimensions $(x, y)$ give the **maximum area**?

### Step 1: Define the Area Equation

A rectangle's area is length times width. In this case, the width is $x$ and the height is $y$.


$$A = x \cdot y$$

Since the rectangle must touch the curve, we know that $y = 3 - x^2$. Let's substitute that into our area formula so we only have one variable:


$$A(x) = x(3 - x^2)$$

$$A(x) = 3x - x^3$$

### Step 2: Find the Derivative ($A'$)

To find the maximum area, we need to find where the "rate of change" of the area is zero.


$$A'(x) = 3 - 3x^2$$

### Step 3: Solve for $x$

Set the derivative to zero:


$$3 - 3x^2 = 0$$

$$3 = 3x^2$$

$$1 = x^2$$

$$\mathbf{x = 1}$$


*(We ignore $x = -1$ because the problem specifies the first quadrant).*

### Step 4: Find the Height ($y$)

Now, plug $x = 1$ back into our curve equation to find the corresponding height:


$$y = 3 - (1)^2$$

$$\mathbf{y = 2}$$

**Result:**
The dimensions for the maximum area are a **width of 1** and a **height of 2**.
